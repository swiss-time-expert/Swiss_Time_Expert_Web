1. Block Diagram of the Mechanical Movement

The block diagram defines the fundamental functional architecture of the manual-winding mechanical watch. The accumulated energy is transmitted, distributed, and regulated continuously:

Step 1: Energy

The Mainspring & Barrel

The watch's engine. The mainspring, housed inside the drum (1), stores mechanical energy during winding.

Step 2: Transmission

The Go-Train (Wheel Train)

Force reduction and multiplication. The gear train (2) routes the energy forward.

Step 3: Distribution

The Escapement

The kinematic converter. The escape wheel (3) transforms continuous rotational movement into impulses.

Step 4: Regulation

The Oscillator

The isochronous heart. The balance-spring assembly (4) dictates the absolute rhythm.

Constant Force: The Fusee-Chain

This historical mechanism acts like a continuously variable transmission to compensate for the mainspring's torque loss as it unwinds.

  • Maximum winding: The spring is strong; the chain pulls on the small diameter of the fusee (short lever arm).
  • Unwinding: The spring weakens; the chain pulls on the large diameter of the fusee (long lever arm), perfectly equalizing the force transmitted to the escapement.

5.1 In-Depth Study of Wheels and Tooth Profile

Detailed analysis of transmission components highlights strict geometric and tribological requirements to ensure optimal energy transfer efficiency (generally above 90% per wheel train level).

5.1.1 The Tooth Profile (Cycloidal Profile)

Unlike industrial engineering which favors the involute tooth profile, Swiss watchmaking traditionally uses the cycloidal profile (specifically epicycloids for wheel teeth and hypocycloids for pinion flanks). This choice fulfills specific constraints:

📷 WORKSHOP 3D MODELING — Close-up view of engagement: geometric interaction between wheel teeth and pinion leaves.
  • Minimised Friction: Cycloidal engagement minimizes surface sliding during tooth entry and release. There is no contact on the line of centers.
  • Tolerance to Center Distance Variations: Watch gear trains operate without heavy lubrication on intermediate trains; the profile tolerates slight variations in center distance without locking the train.

5.1.2 Rigorous Calculations of Transmission Ratios

To ensure kinematic precision, ratio calculations are based on the exact number of teeth Z on wheels and leaves N on pinions:

1° Calculation of Theoretical Power Reserve

Parameters: Barrel = 84 teeth | Center pinion = 12 leaves | Useful turns = 6.5

Ratio (i) = 84 / 12 = 7
Total Autonomy = 7 × 6.5 = 45.5 hours

2° Global Ratio: From Center Wheel to Escapement

  • Center wheel → Third pinion: 80 / 10 = 8
  • Third wheel → Fourth pinion: 75 / 10 = 7.5
  • Fourth wheel → Escape pinion: 70 / 7 = 10
Intermediate Global Ratio = 8 × 7.5 × 10 = 600

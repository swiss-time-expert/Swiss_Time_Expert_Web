Chronological Timeline: From Origins to the Modern Era

📜 Historical Milestones & Inventions

Introduction to Chronometric Evolution

The history of time measurement is an incessant quest for precision, miniaturization, and autonomy. From imposing medieval public clocks to contemporary ultra-thin calibers, every major technical invention has redefined our relationship with time and pushed the limits of micromechanics.

Fundamental Chronology of Mechanical and Electronic Horology

Workshop Masterclass: The Tourbillon by Abraham-Louis Breguet (1801)

Patented on June 26, 1801 (7 Messidor, Year IX), the tourbillon is the masterwork of Abraham-Louis Breguet. Contrary to common misconception, it is not a display complication (it adds no functions to the dial), but a supreme regulation device.

1. The Physical Problem: Gravity

During Breguet's time, worn watches were primarily pocket watches. Placed vertically in a waistcoat pocket, the watch experienced Earth's gravitational pull, which altered the center of gravity of the balance-spring assembly and created permanent rate variations depending on the position.

2. The Mechanical Solution: The Rotating Cage

Breguet conceived enclosing the entire regulating organ (escapement, pallet lever, balance wheel, and hairspring) inside a mobile cage that completes a full rotation every minute. By rotating continuously through 360 degrees, positional errors cancel each other out and average out, guaranteeing perfect isochronism regardless of the watch's vertical position.

⚙️ The Execution Challenge

Crafting a tourbillon requires manufacturing dozens of microscopic components assembled in a cage whose total weight often does not exceed a few tenths of a gram, so as not to burden the movement's moment of inertia. This is why it remains the absolute symbol of mastery in Haute Horlogerie.
